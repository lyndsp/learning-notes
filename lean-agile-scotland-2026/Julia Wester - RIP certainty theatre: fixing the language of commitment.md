# Julia Wester - RIP certainty theatre: fixing the language of commitment

[Synopsis](https://leanagile.scot/programme/rip-certainty-theatre-fixing-language-commitment)

There is a difference between a forecast and a commitment:

- A forecast is what we currently expect.
- A commitment is what we are agreeing to do.

Continuous review connects these.

## Forecasting

When we give a number (e.g. 70%), we need to also provide:

- The evidence used, e.g. historical throughput
- How the evidence was used, e.g. Monte Carlo simulation
- Assumptions made, e.g. future work will be similar to historical work, capacity will continue as-is, no changes in workflow, dependencies, interupts.

Using Monte Carlo simulation and historical delivery data (e.g. how many work-items are delivered each week) allows us to give a percentage forecast, e.g. based on historical data, there is a 90% probability that X work-items will be completed in Y weeks.

If conditions change that invalidate assumptions, replanning and reforecasting is required.

## Commitments

When we make a commitment, consider the form:

- What are we commiting to
- By when
- What is the fallback should our forecast prove incorrect (i.e. risks and mitigations)?
